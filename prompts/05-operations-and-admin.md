# Operations & Admin

Admin is the work that expands to fill whatever time the real work leaves behind. These prompts impose a shape on it: one page for the week, a checklist for a job you repeat, a triage rule for the inbox, a written reason for the tasks you should stop doing.

## 28. My Week On One Page

**When to use:** It is Monday morning, everything feels urgent, and you cannot tell which of the twelve things in your head actually has to happen this week.

**Prompt:**

```text
Turn my brain dump into a realistic plan for the week. Do not add work I did not mention, and do not assume I have more hours than I said.

Everything on my mind, in any order, messy is fine: [BRAIN DUMP - JOBS, MESSAGES TO SEND, QUOTES, ERRANDS, ADMIN, PERSONAL THINGS]
Jobs already booked, with days and times: [BOOKED WORK]
Hours available for everything else after booked work: [REALISTIC HOURS, E.G. 10 HOURS]
Things with real deadlines this week: [DEADLINES OR "none"]
Things that have been on this list for more than two weeks: [OR "not sure"]
Anything I am avoiding: [OR "nothing"]

Give me:

1. A one-page plan, in three blocks: Must happen this week (with the day), Should happen if the week goes well, and Deliberately not this week. Every item from my brain dump must appear in exactly one block - nothing dropped silently.
2. For each "must" item: the single next physical action (for example "ring the merchant about the invoice", not "deal with invoice").
3. Your honest assessment: how many hours this plan needs, and what has to give if it exceeds the time I have.
4. The two items you would move to a "waiting on someone else" list, and what I should do to chase them without it sitting in my head.
5. One question I should answer before Monday is over, because my week depends on it.

Nothing here should be a plan for changing my habits. It is a plan for this week only.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[BRAIN DUMP - ...]` - everything, including personal errands. Half the reason a plan fails is that it ignores the school run.
- `[BOOKED WORK]` - fixed commitments first, so the plan is built on truth.
- `[REALISTIC HOURS, E.G. 10 HOURS]` - be honest. If you have ten spare hours, say ten.
- `[DEADLINES OR "none"]` - real dates only.
- `[OR "not sure"]` and `[OR "nothing"]` - items that keep rolling over, and anything you are avoiding, usually point at something worth deciding.

**Tip:** Print the one-pager or keep it as a single note you look at, not a task app you have to maintain. At the end of the week, run prompt 47 with the plan still in front of you - reviewing against a written plan is quick, reviewing from memory never happens.

## 29. Turn A Job I Repeat Into A Checklist

**When to use:** You do the same kind of job regularly, and you still forget the same step once every few months - usually the one the customer notices.

**Prompt:**

```text
Write a checklist for a job I do repeatedly, from what I tell you, and find the gaps.

The job, in general terms: [WHAT THE JOB IS]
How often I do it: [FREQUENCY]
The steps I actually take, in order, however roughly I remember them: [YOUR STEPS, INCLUDING THE ONES YOU SKIP WHEN BUSY]
Things I have forgotten before, or got wrong at the end of a long day: [REAL MISTAKES YOU REMEMBER]
What the customer always asks me at the end: [THE QUESTION THAT TELLS YOU SOMETHING WAS NOT EXPLAINED]
Tools, materials, information or access I need before starting: [YOUR DEPENDENCIES]
Anything that must never be skipped, for safety, legal or quality reasons: [OR "nothing"]

Give me:

1. A checklist in order, with a heading per stage (before I leave, on arrival, during, before I finish, after I have gone). Use the imperative and short lines.
2. A separate short "before I even quote" section, if any of my dependencies should be resolved before committing.
3. A list of the steps I probably left out, marked GUESS, with a question for each so I can confirm or delete. Do not present guesses as certainties.
4. The three steps most likely to be skipped when I am in a hurry, and a way to make each one hard to skip.
5. A five-line version to keep on my phone, in a sensible order, for when I do not have time to read the full thing.

Do not invent legal, safety or certification requirements - if you think one applies, ask me a question instead of stating it.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[YOUR STEPS, INCLUDING THE ONES YOU SKIP WHEN BUSY]` - include the sloppy version. The gaps are where the value is.
- `[REAL MISTAKES YOU REMEMBER]` - each one becomes a checkpoint. This is the fastest way to make the checklist genuinely yours.
- `[THE QUESTION THAT TELLS YOU SOMETHING WAS NOT EXPLAINED]` - often reveals a missing handover step.
- `[YOUR DEPENDENCIES]` - anything you need before starting.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - mandatory steps you cannot risk leaving to memory.

**Tip:** Keep the checklist as a note you can copy into a phone on the day, and edit it once after the next two or three times you use it - the first draft always has a step that does not survive contact with the real job. If you ever hand this work to someone else, this checklist plus prompt 43 is most of a handover.

## 30. Three Rules That Stop The Inbox Piling Up

**When to use:** You are answering email at eleven at night, or worse, you are not answering it and enquiries are going cold in there.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me set up a simple triage routine for my messages, one that a busy one-person business will actually keep.

How messages reach me: [EMAIL, TEXTS, WHATSAPP, DMs, PHONE CALLS, POST]
Roughly how many a day: [YOUR GUESS IS FINE]
What actually hides in there: [ENQUIRIES, QUOTES, SUPPLIERS, INVOICES, JUNK, CUSTOMER QUESTIONS, ADMIN FROM AUTHORITIES]
What I have missed before: [EG. AN ENQUIRY I FOUND THREE WEEKS LATE]
Times of day I can realistically deal with messages: [E.G. 8-8.30AM AND 5-5.30PM]
What I want to stop doing: [E.G. READING EVERYTHING AND REPLYING TO NOTHING]
Tools I already have: [EMAIL APP, NOTES, PAPER, CALENDAR]

Give me:

1. Three triage rules, each one sentence, that decide what happens to a message in under ten seconds. Rules must be about the message, not about my willpower.
2. A twice-a-day routine: what I do in the first ten minutes and the last ten minutes, step by step.
3. A short list of the message types that should never be answered immediately, and what to do with them instead.
4. The one folder, list or note to keep for "waiting on someone else", and how to review it without it becoming another inbox.
5. What to do about a message that has already been sitting for weeks - a decision rule for old items, including when to simply write it off.

Do not suggest apps I do not have or a system that needs daily maintenance.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[EMAIL, TEXTS, WHATSAPP, DMs, PHONE CALLS, POST]` - all channels matter, because the real inbox is wherever you feel behind.
- `[YOUR GUESS IS FINE]` - volume shapes the rules; an approximate number is enough.
- `[ENQUIRIES, QUOTES, SUPPLIERS, INVOICES, JUNK, ...]` - the mix is what the rules sort into.
- `[EG. AN ENQUIRY I FOUND THREE WEEKS LATE]` - the expensive misses.
- `[E.G. 8-8.30AM AND 5-5.30PM]` - two fixed slots work better than "when I get to it".
- `[EMAIL APP, NOTES, PAPER, CALENDAR]` - the routine must fit the tools you already open every day.

**Tip:** Run the routine for one week with a timer and note how long each block really takes. The most common failure is trying to clear the inbox instead of triaging it - the goal is that nothing urgent is ever more than half a day old. Any enquiry you find late, use prompt 13 to restart the conversation.

## 31. Decide What To Stop Doing

**When to use:** You are busy every day and the business is not moving, because half your hours go on things that do not pay, do not get you customers, and nobody would notice if they stopped.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me decide what to stop doing. Be blunt and work only from the list I give you.

Everything I did in the last month, with my honest estimate of hours per week and what each one is for: [LIST - INCLUDE THE THINGS YOU DO OUT OF HABIT OR GUILT]
Money each item brings in, if any: [REVENUE OR "none yet"]
Customer-facing or behind the scenes: [MARK EACH]
Things I have to keep for legal, contractual or safety reasons: [OR "nothing I know of"]
What I actually want more of: [E.G. FEWER, BIGGER JOBS / MORE REPEAT WORK / A QUIETER WEEK]
What I would feel guilty stopping: [OR "nothing"]

Give me:

1. A table: Task, Hours per week, What it earns or enables, Verdict (keep / reduce / stop / hand over / decide later), and a one-line reason.
2. The stop list, ordered, with what actually happens if I stop - in plain terms, including any consequence I might be ignoring.
3. For the top three on the stop list: the sentence or message I use to end it (to a customer, a supplier, a group I volunteered for).
4. One task worth keeping that looks like a waste of time from the numbers, if my own notes suggest it is not - and why.
5. The hours I free up, and the single best use of them, based on what I said I want more of.

Do not tell me to work harder, get up earlier or be more disciplined. The output is about what to cut, not what to endure.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[LIST - INCLUDE THE THINGS YOU DO OUT OF HABIT OR GUILT]` - the honest list. Include the volunteer role and the trade group newsletter.
- `[REVENUE OR "none yet"]` - if the answer is "none yet", that is important information, not a failure.
- `[MARK EACH]` - so customer-facing work is not cut first by accident.
- `[OR "nothing I know of"]` - obligations that genuinely cannot be dropped.
- `[E.G. FEWER, BIGGER JOBS / MORE REPEAT WORK / A QUIETER WEEK]` - what you want, so the freed hours have a destination.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - the items you would feel guilty about, which are usually the clearest candidates.

**Tip:** Stop one thing this month, not five, and tell the people affected early rather than quietly going silent. After four weeks, use prompt 47 to check whether the freed hours went where you intended - they often drift into the inbox unless you name what they are for.

## 32. Prep For A Conversation I Am Dreading

**When to use:** You have to talk to a supplier, a landlord, a contractor, a difficult customer or a business partner, and you keep putting it off because you do not know how it will go.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me prepare for a difficult conversation. Do not write a script I have to read - prepare me.

Who the conversation is with and what the relationship is: [WHO THEY ARE, HOW LONG YOU HAVE WORKED TOGETHER, HOW MUCH THEY MATTER TO YOU]
What I want to come out of it: [THE IDEAL OUTCOME]
What they want, as far as I can tell: [THEIR INTERESTS]
The facts I am relying on: [DATES, AMOUNTS, WHAT WAS AGREED, WHAT WENT WRONG]
The facts I am not sure about: [OR "nothing"]
What I am afraid will happen: [YOUR WORRY]
Where I am flexible and where I am not: [YOUR LINES]
What I can offer them that costs me little: [OR "nothing"]

Give me:

1. The three sentences I should open with - neutral, factual, no accusation.
2. A table: What they might say, What it really means, What I say back (one or two sentences each).
3. The question I should ask early, and the question I should avoid, with the reason.
4. My walk-away position stated plainly, and the point at which I should end the conversation and continue in writing instead.
5. A short follow-up message to send afterward, recording what was agreed. Include [DATE], [AMOUNT] and [WHO DOES WHAT NEXT] as placeholders.
6. One thing to prepare that has nothing to do with the words - for example the numbers to have in front of me.

Do not encourage threats, exaggerations or invented deadlines. If my position is weak, say so and tell me what that means for how I open.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[WHO THEY ARE, HOW LONG ...]` - the relationship sets the tone.
- `[THE IDEAL OUTCOME]` and `[THEIR INTERESTS]` - knowing what they want is what makes the table useful.
- `[DATES, AMOUNTS, WHAT WAS AGREED, WHAT WENT WRONG]` - facts only. Where you are unsure, say so.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - anything you are hazy about should be checked before the conversation, not during it.
- `[YOUR WORRY]` - naming it lets the preparation cover it.
- `[YOUR LINES]` and `[OR "nothing"]` - flexibility and limits decide the outcome.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - offers that cost you little.

**Tip:** Have the facts on one sheet in front of you and the table ready for the two outcomes you fear most. Send the follow-up message the same day - a conversation with no written trace gets remembered differently, often less favourably to you.

## 33. A Thirty-Minute Weekly Review Worth Doing

**When to use:** Weeks blur into each other, you cannot remember what you actually did, and you have no idea whether the business is going anywhere - only that you are tired.

**Prompt:**

```text
Set up a thirty-minute weekly review I will actually run, and give me this week's version to fill in.

My business and what I am trying to build: [WHAT YOU SELL AND WHAT YOU WANT IN A YEAR]
What I did this week, roughly: [JOBS, ENQUIRIES, QUOTES SENT, MONEY IN, MONEY OUT, ANYTHING PAINFUL]
Numbers I can actually track without extra work: [E.G. JOBS DONE, QUOTES SENT, HOURS WORKED, CASH IN, UNPAID INVOICES]
What tends to get lost between weeks: [EG. FOLLOW-UPS, ENQUIRIES, UNPAID BILLS]
Minutes I really have: [E.G. 30 ON FRIDAY AT 4PM]

Give me:

1. A fixed agenda for the thirty minutes, in timed steps, where every step uses numbers from my list - no step that depends on remembering feelings.
2. This week's scorecard as an empty table I can fill in each week, with a column for last week and a column for "worth a look if".
3. Three questions to answer at the end of each review, each answerable in one line, which together tell me whether to change something next week.
4. The one review output that must be written down, and where to keep it, so next week's review has something to compare against.
5. What to skip when I only have ten minutes, and what never to skip.

Keep every number either something I already track or something I can count in under two minutes. Do not add metrics that need new software.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[WHAT YOU SELL AND WHAT YOU WANT IN A YEAR]` - the review needs a direction to be measuring against.
- `[JOBS, ENQUIRIES, QUOTES SENT, MONEY IN, MONEY OUT, ANYTHING PAINFUL]` - this week's raw material.
- `[E.G. JOBS DONE, QUOTES SENT, HOURS WORKED, CASH IN, UNPAID INVOICES]` - the numbers you can get without effort. Anything needing a new process will not survive.
- `[EG. FOLLOW-UPS, ENQUIRIES, UNPAID BILLS]` - the things that go missing when there is no review.
- `[E.G. 30 ON FRIDAY AT 4PM]` - the actual slot, in your calendar.

**Tip:** Run it in the same slot for four weeks before deciding whether it works, and keep every review on one page so you can read four in a row - the pattern is the point, not any single week. The "worth a look if" thresholds will start telling you when to raise prices, when to chase work, and when to stop worrying.

## 34. A Booking Flow That Writes Its Own Messages

**When to use:** Bookings and changes are managed in your head and your sent folder, so every reschedule turns into four messages you have to think about while also doing the job.

**Prompt:**

```text
Write the standard messages for how I take, confirm and change bookings, so I stop composing them from scratch every time.

What I sell and how long a typical job takes: [OFFER AND TYPICAL DURATION]
How people book with me: [PHONE, TEXT, FORM, EMAIL, IN PERSON, ONLINE CALENDAR]
What I must know before confirming: [DEPOSIT, ACCESS, MATERIALS, TIMING, WHO WILL BE THERE]
Rules I apply, told plainly: [DEPOSITS, CANCELLATION NOTICE, LATE CHANGES, WEATHER OR SUPPLY DELAYS, WAITING TIME]
How I handle a double booking or a delay: [YOUR REAL APPROACH]
When I usually need to change something myself: [OR "rarely"]

Give me these messages, each under 100 words, ready to copy with placeholders marked in [SQUARE BRACKETS]:

1. Confirmation, including what I still need from them.
2. A reminder the day before.
3. A confirmation for a change of date, where the change is mine.
4. A change they asked for, including what it does to the price or the wait, if anything.
5. A delay on the day, apologetic but concrete.
6. A no-show or a repeat cancellation.
7. A short message for a first-time customer, explaining what to expect on the day.

Then: tell me the two messages where my rules are unclear and I should decide before I need them, and mark any line where I would be making a promise I did not give you.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[OFFER AND TYPICAL DURATION]` - so reminders and timings are realistic.
- `[PHONE, TEXT, FORM, EMAIL, IN PERSON, ONLINE CALENDAR]` - the channel decides length.
- `[DEPOSIT, ACCESS, MATERIALS, TIMING, WHO WILL BE THERE]` - the facts you need to avoid a wasted visit.
- `[DEPOSITS, CANCELLATION NOTICE, LATE CHANGES, WEATHER OR SUPPLY DELAYS, WAITING TIME]` - your actual rules, not the ones you wish you had.
- `[YOUR REAL APPROACH]` - how you really handle it, since that is what goes in the message.
- `[OR "rarely"]` - be honest if delays are common; the wording needs to be different.

**Tip:** Put messages 1, 2 and 4 into your phone's shortcuts today; they are the ones sent most often. If a rule needs deciding, decide it once and use prompt 25 to write it in plain English so the same answer goes to every customer, every time.
