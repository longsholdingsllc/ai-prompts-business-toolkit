# Money & Numbers

None of these prompts give financial advice, do your bookkeeping or file anything - they help you get your own numbers into a shape you can make a decision with. Everything they produce is a draft for you to check, and anything to do with tax, contracts or debt belongs with someone qualified.

## 35. What An Hour Of My Time Has To Earn

**When to use:** You price jobs by feel, or by what you charged last year, and you are not sure whether the price actually covers your week.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me work out what an hour of my working time has to earn, using only my own numbers. This is a planning exercise, not financial advice - show me the working so I can check it.

What I need to take out of the business each month to live, before tax: [PERSONAL AMOUNT]
Business costs per month, listed: [RENT, INSURANCE, SOFTWARE, VEHICLE, PHONE, SUBSCRIPTIONS, MATERIALS, ANYTHING REGULAR]
Costs that come once or twice a year: [INSURANCE, TAX BILL, EQUIPMENT, ACCOUNTANT]
Hours I can actually sell in a normal week: [REALISTIC BILLABLE HOURS - NOT THE HOURS YOU WORK]
Weeks I expect to work this year: [NUMBER, ALLOWING FOR HOLIDAYS AND QUIET PERIODS]
What I charged on my last three jobs and how long each took: [JOB, PRICE, HOURS]
Money currently owed to me and when it is due: [OR "nothing outstanding"]

Do this:

1. A table of the monthly and yearly costs, totalling each, with a note for anything I listed that looks like it is probably a guess.
2. The hourly figure my week has to produce to cover my costs and my pay, showing each step of the arithmetic so I can recalculate it myself if a number changes.
3. The same figure as a day rate and as a weekly target, because those are easier to feel.
4. What my last three jobs actually earned per hour, next to the figure from step 2, and which of the three I should stop repeating.
5. The three numbers in my list that would change this most if I was wrong about them, so I know what to check first.
6. Anything in my list that suggests a problem I should look at, stated as a question rather than a conclusion.

Ask me for any missing figure instead of estimating it.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[PERSONAL AMOUNT]` - what you need to live on each month. Before tax.
- `[RENT, INSURANCE, SOFTWARE, ...]` - every regular business cost, including the annoying small ones.
- `[INSURANCE, TAX BILL, EQUIPMENT, ACCOUNTANT]` - annual costs, divided across the year by the assistant.
- `[REALISTIC BILLABLE HOURS - NOT THE HOURS YOU WORK]` - this is the number people get wrong: quoting, travel, admin and invoices are not billable.
- `[NUMBER, ALLOWING FOR HOLIDAYS AND QUIET PERIODS]` - not 52.
- `[JOB, PRICE, HOURS]` - three recent jobs, with hours as accurately as you can remember.
- `[OR "nothing outstanding"]` - unpaid invoices, if any.

**Tip:** Write the hourly figure on a card and check the next quote against it before sending. Re-run the prompt any time a big cost changes or your quiet season starts, and use prompt 36 when you need to turn the figure into a price for a specific job. For anything tax-related, take these numbers to your accountant rather than acting on them directly.

## 36. Price A New Service Without Guessing

**When to use:** You are adding a service, a product or a package and have no idea what to charge - only a vague feeling that the number you picked is either too high or too low.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me price [THE THING] using my own numbers and some judgement about what the price signals. This is a planning exercise, not financial advice.

What I am pricing: [DESCRIBE THE SERVICE, PACKAGE OR PRODUCT]
What it costs me to deliver, in hours and in materials: [YOUR ESTIMATE, ROUGH IS FINE]
What similar work I already sell costs: [EXISTING PRICES]
What I charge per hour as a minimum: [YOUR FIGURE FROM PROMPT 35, OR "I do not know"]
Who it is for and what they get: [BUYER AND OUTCOME]
What I want out of it: [E.G. EASY VOLUME WORK / HIGH VALUE, FEWER CLIENTS / ENTRY POINT THAT LEADS TO MORE]
Competitor or substitute prices, only the ones I actually know: [OR "I do not know any"]

Do this:

1. A floor calculation: what the price must be to cover my hours and costs at my minimum hourly figure. Show the arithmetic.
2. Three price points above that floor - low, middle, high - and for each: what a buyer would assume about the scope and quality at that price, how many I would need to sell to make it worth doing, and what would have to be true for it to work.
3. What the middle option should include and exclude so that it is genuinely worth the difference from the low one, not just the same thing at a higher number.
4. A table: Price, Who it suits, What it signals, Main risk.
5. The one question I should ask two real customers before setting the final number, and the honest sign that I have priced it too low.
6. A boundary I should set at launch, for example what happens if demand or delivery takes twice as long as expected.

Do not invent competitor prices, conversion rates or market data. Where you do not have a number, ask me for it or mark it as unknown.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[DESCRIBE THE SERVICE, PACKAGE OR PRODUCT]` - what exactly is being sold.
- `[YOUR ESTIMATE, ROUGH IS FINE]` - hours and materials per delivery.
- `[EXISTING PRICES]` - so the new thing sits sensibly next to what you already sell.
- `[YOUR FIGURE FROM PROMPT 35, OR "I do not know"]` - run prompt 35 first if you do not have it.
- `[BUYER AND OUTCOME]` - who buys and what they get.
- `[E.G. EASY VOLUME WORK / HIGH VALUE, FEWER CLIENTS / ENTRY POINT THAT LEADS TO MORE]` - the purpose changes the right price.
- `[OR "I do not know any"]` - honest option. Do not let anyone invent a competitor price for you.

**Tip:** Launch at the middle price with the boundaries from step 6 written down, then review after five sales instead of five days. If everything sells instantly, the price was too low; if nothing does, check whether people understood the offer before you touch the number - prompt 4 is the better tool for that.

## 37. Which Costs To Cut First

**When to use:** Money is tight this month, every cost feels necessary, and you are about to cancel the wrong subscriptions while ignoring the expensive thing.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me rank my costs for cutting. Work only from the list I give you and do not assume anything is unimportant.

My monthly income, averaged over the last three months: [AMOUNT OR "variable, roughly X to Y"]
Everything I pay for regularly, with the monthly amount and how long I have had it: [LIST - INCLUDE SUBSCRIPTIONS, SERVICES, SUPPLIES, FEES, LOANS, ANYTHING REGULAR]
Annual or irregular costs: [LIST WITH AMOUNTS]
Costs tied to a contract, a notice period or a penalty: [OR "nothing"]
What each cost is for, in your own words: [ONE LINE PER ITEM IF YOU CAN]
What I cannot cut without breaking the business: [OR "nothing"]

Give me:

1. A table: Item, Monthly cost, Annual cost, What it is for, Cut / Reduce / Renegotiate / Keep, Difficulty of changing it, and a one-line reason.
2. Order it by money saved per hour of effort, so the quick wins are obvious.
3. The three cuts that free the most money with the least disruption, each with the exact step I take to make it happen (including the wording for an email asking to reduce or cancel, if the cost is a service).
4. The costs that look expensive but earn their place, and why - so I do not cut something that brings in work.
5. The total saved, and a plain note of what that buys me - weeks of runway, a month of slack, or very little, stated honestly.
6. One cost worth renegotiating rather than cancelling, and the questions to ask when I do.

Do not tell me to cut costs I did not list, and do not suggest tax or legal arrangements - those need someone qualified.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[AMOUNT OR "variable, roughly X to Y"]` - what actually comes in.
- `[LIST - INCLUDE SUBSCRIPTIONS, SERVICES, SUPPLIES, FEES, LOANS, ...]` - the full list, including the small ones and the ones you have forgotten you pay for.
- `[LIST WITH AMOUNTS]` - annual costs hit differently from monthly ones.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - contractual commitments matter; cutting them costs more than it saves.
- `[ONE LINE PER ITEM IF YOU CAN]` - the purpose is what tells you whether it earns its place.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - things that literally keep the business working.

**Tip:** Make the cuts from step 3 this week rather than adding them to a list, and put the wording from step 3 somewhere you can find it, because renegotiating the same supplier next year gets easier with a written ask. Where a cost touches a contract, a loan or a tax position, talk to someone qualified before acting.

## 38. What I Owe And What Is Owed To Me

**When to use:** You are not sure whether you are in trouble or just having a normal month, because all the numbers live in different places and your head.

**Prompt:**

```text
Build me a plain picture of my next ninety days. Facts only - I will make the decisions.

Money I expect to come in: [INVOICES DUE WITH DATES, REGULAR WORK, OR "I do not know"]
Money others owe me and when it was due: [CUSTOMER, AMOUNT, DATE DUE]
Regular costs and when they leave my account: [LIST WITH DATES OR DAYS OF THE MONTH]
One-off payments coming up: [TAX, INSURANCE, EQUIPMENT, RENT REVIEW, OR "none"]
Debt or finance payments: [AMOUNT, FREQUENCY, OR "none"]
Money in my account today: [AMOUNT]
Personal drawings I take out: [AMOUNT AND HOW OFTEN]
Anything I am worried about: [YOUR WORRY, OR "nothing specific"]

Give me:

1. A month-by-month table for the next three months: money in, money out, what that leaves, and the lowest point in each month. Mark any line you had to infer as GUESS.
2. The single tightest week in the ninety days and what makes it tight.
3. A list of the invoices or receipts I should chase now, in the order that has the most effect, with the dates I should chase them.
4. What would have to happen for this to become a real problem, described in plain terms - for example a customer paying a month late, or a job not coming off.
5. Three questions I should be able to answer from my own records and currently cannot, so I know what to fix.
6. What to do first if the tightest week gets tighter: the two actions with the fastest effect, in the order I should take them.

Do not estimate amounts I did not give you, and do not recommend borrowing, tax arrangements or investment decisions. If the picture is genuinely tight, say so plainly rather than softening it.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[INVOICES DUE WITH DATES, REGULAR WORK, OR "I do not know"]` - even a guess marked as a guess is more useful than nothing.
- `[CUSTOMER, AMOUNT, DATE DUE]` - your own unpaid invoices, with how overdue they are.
- `[LIST WITH DATES OR DAYS OF THE MONTH]` - when money actually leaves, so the tight week is visible.
- `[TAX, INSURANCE, EQUIPMENT, RENT REVIEW, OR "none"]` - the irregular hits are what catch people out.
- `[AMOUNT, FREQUENCY, OR "none"]` - finance and loan payments.
- `[AMOUNT]`, `[AMOUNT AND HOW OFTEN]` - the starting point and what you take out.
- `[YOUR WORRY, OR "nothing specific"]` - naming it means the answer can address it.

**Tip:** Update this once a month with the same prompt and keep the previous versions; the pattern across three months is far more useful than any single one. If step 4 shows a real risk, run prompt 49 to plan for it before it happens, and start the chasing from step 3 immediately - the oldest invoice is almost always the hardest to collect.

## 39. Chase An Overdue Invoice In Three Steps

**When to use:** An invoice is overdue, you know chasing is necessary, and you keep putting it off because you do not want to sour the relationship.

**Prompt:**

```text
Write my three invoices-chasing messages. Keep them firm, factual and free of anything I cannot back up.

Who owes me: [CUSTOMER TYPE AND RELATIONSHIP]
What the invoice was for: [THE WORK]
Amount and invoice number: [AMOUNT AND REFERENCE]
Date it was due: [DUE DATE]
How overdue: [DAYS OR WEEKS]
What I have already sent: [OR "nothing yet"]
Anything agreed verbally that is not in writing: [OR "nothing"]
Their payment terms as I stated them: [E.G. 14 DAYS, OR "I did not state terms"]
What I want to avoid: [E.G. LOSING THEM, BEING RUDE, GOING TOO SOFT]

Give me:

1. Message 1, first reminder: short, assumes it slipped, restates the facts and asks for a payment date. Under 80 words.
2. Message 2, a week later: firmer, states the consequence that is actually true and that I can carry out (for example pausing further work or charging agreed late fees only if they exist), and asks for a specific date.
3. Message 3, a fortnight after that: a final notice that says plainly what happens next if payment does not arrive, without threats I cannot act on.
4. For each message: the subject line, what to do if they reply with a part payment or a promise, and what to do if they simply do not reply.
5. A note on whether their payment terms, as I described them, are enforceable or clear enough to rely on - if not, tell me that they are not and say what to state in future.
6. One short sentence to add to future invoices, based on what I told you.

Do not invent late fees, legal consequences or interest that were not part of my terms, and do not escalate beyond what I have said I am prepared to do. If anything here looks like it needs legal advice, say so rather than writing it definitively.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[CUSTOMER TYPE AND RELATIONSHIP]` - repeat customer, one-off, or someone you want to keep.
- `[THE WORK]` - what was delivered, so the messages can reference it factually.
- `[AMOUNT AND REFERENCE]` - exact figures. Vagueness weakens every message.
- `[DUE DATE]` and `[DAYS OR WEEKS]` - the timeline is your strongest fact.
- `[OR "nothing yet"]` - the history of chasing.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - verbal arrangements people often "remember" differently.
- `[E.G. 14 DAYS, OR "I did not state terms"]` - honest answer here leads to sound advice in point 5.
- `[E.G. LOSING THEM, BEING RUDE, GOING TOO SOFT]` - what you want to protect.

**Tip:** Send message 1 the day you notice the invoice is late rather than at the end of the month, and keep all three in one thread so there is a dated record. Put the sentence from point 6 on every future invoice - clear terms at the start prevent most of this. If the amount is large or the customer disputes it, take advice before the final notice.

## 40. Read My Numbers Back To Me In Plain English

**When to use:** You have the figures - from your bank app, your spreadsheet, your accountant - and what you actually need is someone to tell you what they mean, without a table you cannot interpret.

**Prompt:**

```text
Read my numbers back to me in plain English. Do not give me financial advice and do not tell me what to do - tell me what the numbers say, what changed, and what you cannot tell from them.

Period covered: [E.G. LAST THREE MONTHS, OR LAST MONTH]
My figures, roughly or exactly, however you have them: [INCOME, EXPENSES, CASH IN THE BANK, INVOICES NOT YET PAID, BEST AND WORST MONTH, ANYTHING ELSE YOU HAVE]
What was unusual in this period: [EG. A BIG JOB, A QUIET MONTH, A ONE-OFF PURCHASE]
What I already think is going on: [YOUR READ]
What I am trying to decide: [E.G. WHETHER TO HIRE, PUT PRICES UP, CUT SOMETHING, TAKE A BREAK]

Give me:

1. Five sentences a friend would understand: what came in, what went out, what is left, what is owed to me, what I owe. Use plain words, not accounting terms.
2. What changed since the previous period, and whether the change is big enough to be meaningful or just a normal wobble.
3. The three things these numbers genuinely do not tell you - the questions I cannot answer from the figures I gave you.
4. The one number that matters most for the decision I am trying to make, and why that one.
5. What I should go and find out before deciding, in the order it matters.
6. Anything in my figures that looks inconsistent or impossible, and the question that would resolve it.

Do not project future revenue, estimate any figure I did not give you, or recommend tax, legal or investment steps.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[E.G. LAST THREE MONTHS, OR LAST MONTH]` - the period, so changes can be compared.
- `[INCOME, EXPENSES, CASH IN THE BANK, INVOICES NOT YET PAID, BEST AND WORST MONTH, ...]` - whatever you have. Rough figures give a rough read, and that is stated in the answer.
- `[EG. A BIG JOB, A QUIET MONTH, A ONE-OFF PURCHASE]` - so a one-off is not read as a trend.
- `[YOUR READ]` - your own interpretation, so the answer can confirm or challenge it.
- `[E.G. WHETHER TO HIRE, PUT PRICES UP, CUT SOMETHING, TAKE A BREAK]` - the decision you are heading towards.

**Tip:** Do this monthly, five minutes with the same three figures, and keep the five sentences from part 1 in one note - reading twelve of them in a row tells you more about your business than any single report. Where part 3 names a question you cannot answer, that is usually the next thing to track. Anything about tax, debt or contracts goes to someone qualified, with these figures in hand.
