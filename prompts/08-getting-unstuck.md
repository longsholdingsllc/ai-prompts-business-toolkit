# Getting Unstuck

Every operator has a decision they keep putting off, a project that is too big to start, and a month they would rather not look at. These five prompts are for those moments. They are written to give you a next step, not a pep talk.

## 46. The Decision I Keep Putting Off

**When to use:** A decision has been sitting in your head for weeks - a price rise, a supplier, a customer you should probably drop, whether to keep going with something - and thinking about it has become a habit.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me make a decision I have been avoiding. Do not decide for me - build the decision so I can decide in ten minutes.

The decision, phrased as a question with two or more options: [E.G. "DO I RAISE PRICES 15% OR KEEP THEM AND TAKE MORE WORK?"]
How long I have been putting it off: [WEEKS OR MONTHS]
Why I have not decided: [YOUR HONEST REASON - FEAR OF LOSING CUSTOMERS, NO TIME, NOT ENOUGH INFORMATION, SOMEONE ELSE'S OPINION]
The facts I am sure of: [LIST]
What I do not know: [LIST]
What happens if I do nothing for another three months: [YOUR BEST GUESS]
Who else is affected: [OR "just me"]
What I would tell a friend in this position: [YOUR ANSWER, OR "I do not know"]
The deadline, real or self-imposed, if there is one: [OR "none"]

Give me:

1. The decision restated in one sentence, with the options listed. Include "do nothing" as an explicit option with its cost.
2. A table: Option, What has to be true for it to be the right choice, What it costs me if I am wrong, How reversible it is, What I would need to know to choose it.
3. The two options that actually survive scrutiny, and why the others are weaker - be willing to say an option is weak.
4. The reversible parts: what I could try on a small scale before committing fully, and what that test would cost.
5. The information that would change the decision, and whether it is worth getting versus just deciding now with what I have.
6. One question to answer before deciding, and what my answer would tell me.

Where my facts are thin, ask me for the missing figure rather than assuming it.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[E.G. "DO I RAISE PRICES 15% OR KEEP THEM AND TAKE MORE WORK?"]` - the decision as a question with real options. Vague framing produces vague output.
- `[WEEKS OR MONTHS]` - how long it has been sitting there.
- `[YOUR HONEST REASON - ...]` - the real reason, not the polite one. It usually points at which risk matters most.
- `[LIST]` and `[LIST]` - what you know and what you do not. Writing the second list is often the decision.
- `[YOUR BEST GUESS]` - the cost of drift, which is the option people forget.
- `[OR "just me"]` - who else is affected, including family or a business partner.
- `[YOUR ANSWER, OR "I do not know"]` - your own instinct, so the answer can confirm or challenge it.
- `[OR "none"]` - real or self-imposed deadlines; both are useful to name.

**Tip:** Decide within twenty-four hours of running this, because the value is in choosing, not in having the perfect answer. If the reversible test in point 4 is cheap, do that instead of deciding at all - a small trial beats another month of thinking. Write your chosen option and the date in a note so that in three months you can check whether it was right.

## 47. Where Did The Week Actually Go

**When to use:** It is Friday, you have worked all week, and you could not say what you got done or whether it moved anything forward.

**Prompt:**

```text
Review my week against what I planned, honestly and without judgement. Facts first, then one change.

What I planned for this week: [PASTE YOUR PLAN OR LIST WHAT YOU INTENDED]
What I actually did, hour by hour where I can remember and roughly where I cannot: [YOUR RECORD - MESSY IS FINE]
Money in and out this week: [AMOUNTS OR "no idea"]
Enquiries, quotes and follow-ups: [NUMBERS OR "I did not track"]
What got pushed to next week, and how many weeks it has been pushed: [LIST]
What went wrong or surprised me: [OR "nothing"]
What I avoided: [OR "nothing"]
Time I actually worked: [ROUGH HOURS]

Give me:

1. A comparison table: Planned, Did, Moved the business forward (yes / no / unclear), and the reason.
2. Where my week actually went, in four or five categories with rough hours, based only on what I told you. Mark anything you inferred as GUESS.
3. The three things I did that will not matter in a month - said plainly, not unkindly.
4. What got pushed again, and the one decision I should make about each rather than pushing it a fourth time.
5. My honest score for the week in one line, and the single most valuable thing I did - which may not be the one that felt productive.
6. One change for next week only. Not a system, not a habit, one change, with the exact thing to do differently.
7. One question to ask myself at the start of next week so this review takes ten minutes instead of thirty.

Do not tell me to be more disciplined. Do not invent numbers I did not give you.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[PASTE YOUR PLAN OR LIST WHAT YOU INTENDED]` - the plan you wrote on Monday, not the one you remember. This is why prompt 28 is worth keeping.
- `[YOUR RECORD - MESSY IS FINE]` - a rough diary, calendar entries, or a list from memory. Accuracy matters more than completeness.
- `[AMOUNTS OR "no idea"]`, `[NUMBERS OR "I did not track"]` - honesty here is the point; "no idea" is itself useful information.
- `[LIST]` - pushed tasks and how often they have been pushed.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - what surprised you, and what you avoided. The avoided item is usually the important one.
- `[ROUGH HOURS]` - the hours you actually worked, not the hours you felt busy.

**Tip:** Run this every Friday in the same ten-minute slot and keep the output, so in six weeks you can read them in one sitting and see patterns no single week shows. Take point 6 seriously and change exactly one thing - five changes at once is how the review itself gets abandoned. If the same task has been pushed four times, that is prompt 46, not a time-management problem.

## 48. Turn This Overwhelming Project Into This Week

**When to use:** Something big needs doing - a new website, quoting system, product, tax catch-up, clearing out a mess - and it has been "starting next week" for two months because it is too large to face.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me start a project I have been avoiding and make a real first week out of it.

What the project is, as I understand it: [DESCRIBE IT BADLY - THAT IS FINE]
Why it matters: [WHAT CHANGES IF IT IS DONE]
Why I have not started: [HONEST REASON - TOO BIG, NO TIME, DO NOT KNOW WHERE TO BEGIN, DO NOT WANT TO]
What already exists: [ANY PROGRESS, FILES, NOTES, OR "nothing"]
The deadline, if there is a real one: [OR "none"]
Hours per week I can give it: [REALISTIC - E.G. THREE ONE-HOUR BLOCKS]
What I need it to be good enough to do: [THE MINIMUM USEFUL OUTCOME, NOT THE DREAM VERSION]
Anything outside my control: [SUPPLIERS, OTHER PEOPLE, SOFTWARE, APPROVALS]

Give me:

1. The project split into stages, each with a clear finish line, in the order they must happen. Note which stage is the one that usually stops people.
2. The smallest first version that would be genuinely useful, and what to leave out of it on purpose.
3. The next five actions, each doable in under sixty minutes, written as physical actions starting with a verb. Each one must be something I can do alone, today or this week.
4. A plan for the first week using my real available hours, and what I will not do that week instead.
5. The two decisions I have to make before starting, and what I would need to know to make each. If a decision can be made either way without much cost, say so - I am not allowed to stall on reversible choices.
6. What to tell other people, if anything, so the project is not quietly sabotaged by normal life.

Do not give me a timeline in months, or a plan that requires work I cannot do alone.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[DESCRIBE IT BADLY - THAT IS FINE]` - an unclear description is enough to start; the first action is often clarifying it.
- `[WHAT CHANGES IF IT IS DONE]` - the reason to push through the middle part.
- `[HONEST REASON - ...]` - the real blocker, so the plan can work around it.
- `[ANY PROGRESS, FILES, NOTES, OR "nothing"]` - so you do not restart from zero.
- `[OR "none"]` - a real deadline changes the plan; a self-imposed one can move.
- `[REALISTIC - E.G. THREE ONE-HOUR BLOCKS]` - hours like this beat "I will do a bit every day".
- `[THE MINIMUM USEFUL OUTCOME, NOT THE DREAM VERSION]` - this is what makes it finishable.
- `[SUPPLIERS, OTHER PEOPLE, SOFTWARE, APPROVALS]` - so you know what to start early.

**Tip:** Do action one from point 3 within the next hour, before you plan anything else, and give the project a fixed slot in the week rather than finding time for it. Finish a stage before improving the previous one, and if you have not touched it for two weeks, come back to point 3 and pick the smallest action on the list instead of replanning from the top.

## 49. Plan For The Month That Goes Badly

**When to use:** You are nervous about the next few months - a quiet season, a big customer leaving, a cost going up - and "I will deal with it if it happens" is not really a plan.

**Prompt:**

```text
Help me plan for a bad month before it happens. I want decisions made now so I am not making them under pressure.

My business and how it earns: [WHAT YOU SELL AND WHO PAYS YOU]
My average monthly income over the last six months: [AMOUNT OR ROUGH RANGE]
The bad scenario I am worried about: [EG. INCOME DROPS 30% FOR TWO MONTHS, A BIG CUSTOMER LEAVES, A KEY COST RISES]
My regular costs, split into what I must pay and what I could pause: [TWO LISTS]
Money I have set aside for exactly this: [AMOUNT OR "nothing"]
Commitments I cannot break: [CONTRACTS, LEASES, STAFF, LOANS, OR "none"]
What I could sell or start quickly if I needed income: [YOUR IDEAS]
Who would be affected by my decisions: [FAMILY, STAFF, SUPPLIERS, OR "just me"]

Give me:

1. Trigger points: how I will know the bad month has started rather than just being a slow week. Give me three concrete triggers I can see in my own numbers, not a feeling.
2. A prepared decision list, in order, in a table: Trigger reached, The decision already made, Who I tell and when, What it costs, How reversible it is.
3. The first three cuts or changes, ranked by how fast they take effect, and the exact step to make each one.
4. What I do first to bring income in, using only the things I said I could start quickly, and how it fits around existing work.
5. What I should NOT do, and why - including the tempting moves that make a bad month worse.
6. One thing to prepare while things are fine: the document or list to have ready so the plan takes an hour to run, not a week.
7. A note on anything in my plan that depends on someone else agreeing, since that is where the timing will slip.

Do not recommend borrowing, selling assets, or taking on debt. If the plan looks like it will not be enough, say so plainly and tell me what that means.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[WHAT YOU SELL AND WHO PAYS YOU]` - the revenue shape.
- `[AMOUNT OR ROUGH RANGE]` - the normal baseline, so a "30 per cent drop" can be made concrete.
- `[EG. INCOME DROPS 30% ...]` - name the specific scenario. A vague fear produces a vague plan.
- `[TWO LISTS]` - what must be paid versus what could be paused. This distinction does most of the work.
- `[AMOUNT OR "nothing"]` - your buffer, honestly.
- `[CONTRACTS, LEASES, STAFF, LOANS, OR "none"]` - what cannot be cut.
- `[YOUR IDEAS]` - income options you would actually consider.
- `[FAMILY, STAFF, SUPPLIERS, OR "just me"]` - the plan affects people, and that shapes what you prepare.

**Tip:** Prepare point 6 now, save it somewhere you will find it in a bad week, and read it once a quarter so it stays current. When a trigger is reached, run the decision list in order rather than inventing a new plan at 11pm. Anything involving contracts, debt or employment obligations needs professional advice, and getting it before the trigger is far cheaper than getting it after.

## 50. Argue Against My Own Plan

**When to use:** You have a plan, an idea, a launch or a big decision you feel good about - and nobody around you is going to test it properly.

**Prompt:**

```text
Be the strongest honest critic of my plan. Your job is to find the weakest parts, not to encourage me.

The plan, in full: [DESCRIBE IT - WHAT I WILL DO, FOR WHOM, OVER WHAT PERIOD]
What has to be true for it to work: [YOUR ASSUMPTIONS, STATED AS ASSUMPTIONS]
My evidence for each assumption: [WHAT YOU KNOW VERSUS WHAT YOU BELIEVE]
What it costs me: [TIME, MONEY, OPPORTUNITY]
What I would stop doing to make room for it: [OR "nothing"]
What success looks like at 30, 90 and 180 days, in numbers I can actually check: [YOUR FIGURES]
Who benefits if I am right: [WHO PAYS YOU AND WHY THEY WOULD]
What I have already spent or committed: [OR "nothing"]
How I will know if it is failing: [YOUR ANSWER, OR "I have not decided"]

Do this in five parts:

1. The five weakest assumptions, ranked by how much damage it does if the assumption is wrong. Mark each one: could I test it cheaply, or am I betting on it?
2. The single thing most likely to kill this plan, and the early warning sign I would see first.
3. A pre-mortem: it is nine months from now and this failed. Write three short, plausible stories of how, told in the past tense.
4. What my plan looks like to someone who is not being kind - including whether it is a plan or a wish, and what would move it from one to the other.
5. The three changes that would make the plan considerably more robust, each one sentence, and one thing I should keep exactly as it is because it is genuinely sound.

Use only what I gave you. Do not invent market data, competitor behaviour or statistics, and do not soften your criticism to be polite. If the plan is sound, say so, but only after looking hard.
```

**Fill in:**

- `[DESCRIBE IT - WHAT I WILL DO, FOR WHOM, OVER WHAT PERIOD]` - enough detail to be argued with.
- `[YOUR ASSUMPTIONS, STATED AS ASSUMPTIONS]` - listing them yourself is half the exercise.
- `[WHAT YOU KNOW VERSUS WHAT YOU BELIEVE]` - this distinction is what part 1 ranks on.
- `[TIME, MONEY, OPPORTUNITY]` - all three costs, including what else you could do with the time.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - what you will stop to make room.
- `[YOUR FIGURES]` - checkable numbers, not adjectives.
- `[WHO PAYS YOU AND WHY THEY WOULD]` - if you cannot answer this, that is the finding.
- `[OR "nothing"]` - money or time already committed.
- `[YOUR ANSWER, OR "I have not decided"]` - how you will spot failure, which is often missing from plans.

**Tip:** Run this before you spend money, and once more halfway through - the second run usually exposes a different weakness, because by then you know things you did not know at the start. Decide in advance what early warning sign would make you stop, write it down, and then answer part 4 honestly. If the plan survives this without changes, you either wrote it well or asked too gently; try prompt 47 four weeks in, which tests the plan against what actually happened.
